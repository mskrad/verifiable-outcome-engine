Commit, prepare, settle, replay.

The rules go on-chain first; the randomness comes from blocks that do not exist yet. The operator cannot change the rules after the commit or know the winner before the target slots exist.

Commit

Participants, weights and formula are stored on-chain under a SHA-256 artifact hash and bound to a runtime.

finalize_compiled_artifact

Prepare

Opens the draw and fixes three target slots 8, 16 and 24 ahead, in three different leader groups.

prepare_rng_v2 · +8 · +16 · +24

Settle

Reads the three slot hashes from SlotHashes, hashes them with the rules into a seed and writes the winners.

settle_rng_v2

Replay

Anyone rebuilds the artifact from chain data, recomputes the seed from the recorded slot hashes and gets the same winners.

What a MATCH proves.

        
And what it does not. The full boundary for every path is in the spec.

      

      

        
Proves
  • The rules did not change: participants, weights, formula and winner count are the exact bytes committed on-chain.
  • The winners follow from the rules and the recorded randomness, in the same order.
  • Event, resolution account and replay agree on inputs, outputs and winner ids.

        
Does not prove
  • That the list was fair or complete when committed, only that it did not change after.
  • That the operator could not foresee the result on every path. That depends on the randomness source.
  • That a prize was paid. Payout is the integrator’s step.
