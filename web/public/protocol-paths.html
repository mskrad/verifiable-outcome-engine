Resolution paths

Resolution paths, one program.

Draws use single-phase rng_v2: the rules are committed, three future slots are fixed, and the winners come from their hashes. Every path below runs on the same canonical devnet program, and any resolution transaction can be replayed from its signature.

Scope

Everything on this page runs on Solana devnet, on the canonical program 6QtLot…CP6B. Nothing is deployed to mainnet yet — these statuses compare paths against each other, not against a mainnet release.

single phase v1.2 canonical · default prepare_rng_v2 → settle_rng_v2. Winners from three future slot hashes; used by every draw endpoint. Try it →
two phase v1.3.2 canonical · opt-in seal_randomness → attest_winners. Anyone may seal; the operator stakes a bounty and cannot back out.

unicorn-g entropy B8 implemented · not deployed Alternative entropy source: a delay-function output verified on-chain through an embedded Groth16 verifier. The canonical program is not built with it.
Unicorn-G — why it is not deployed

It is implemented and was measured on devnet in June 2026: seal_randomness_unicorn verifies the proof on-chain, and a forged one is rejected with InvalidUnicornGProof at the pairing check. The canonical program is not built with it. Before mainnet it needs the circuit soundness fix and a trusted-setup ceremony.

Conditions

Live on the canonical program since 2026-07-03. The public scorer POST /api/conditions/score is byte-identical to on-chain resolve_conditions, and a devnet attestation replays to MATCH. Payout is pro-rata over the winners and executed by the integrator — the engine proves who won, nothing more. Score a round →

Sortition by claim.

A panel, a jury or a whitelist chosen from sealed tickets. Nobody knows who is selected until the selected person claims, and the operator never holds the secrets.

  1. Sealed tickets

    Each entrant registers a hash of a secret only they know. Nobody, the operator included, can tell what is inside.

    register · sha256(secret ‖ ticket ‖ draw)

  2. Omega from future slots

    Registration closes first. Then three slot hashes, 75, 90 and 105 slots after creation, fix one number: omega.

    seal_sortition_draw

  3. Every ticket gets a point

    The secret and omega place each ticket on a line. Only its owner can compute where.

    point = sha256(secret ‖ omega)

  4. Winners claim

    Points below the threshold win. They show up only when their owner claims; the rest stay sealed.

    claim_sortition_ticket · ~8,200 CU
Illustration: threshold set for 10 expected winners of 100. Simulated over 200 draws × 100 tickets: mean 9.7.

Unicorn-G entropy.

For draws where even the slot leader must not be able to steer the result. implemented · not deployed

  1. Slot hashes in

    Three future slot hashes are mixed into one input, u.

    u ← SlotHashes

  2. A delay nobody can skip

    A VDF is a long chain of sequential squarings. Extra machines do not finish it sooner, so no one knows the output while it could still be changed.

    y = VDF(u)

  3. A 256-byte proof

    A Groth16 proof shows that the Wesolowski check for u and y passes.

    π · 256 bytes

  4. Checked in one transaction

    The program verifies the proof on-chain and takes randomness from y. A forged proof is rejected.

    247,476 CU · InvalidUnicornGProof
Measured on devnet, June 2026. The canonical program is not built with this feature; mainnet needs a circuit fix and a trusted setup.
